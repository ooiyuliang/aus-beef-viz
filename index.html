From Paddock to Plate

The beef in many Malaysian supermarkets and restaurants comes from Australia, one of the world's biggest beef exporters. Where are Australia's cattle raised, and how has the industry changed?

A beef giant on the world stage

Australia is the world's fifth-largest beef producer, making about 2.6 million tonnes of beef in 2024. Only the United States, Brazil, China and Argentina produce more. Malaysia, by comparison, produced about 39,000 tonnes and ranked 103rd.

Each circle's size shows how much beef a country produces. Switch between years to see how the picture has changed: Brazil's production grew by 61% between 2005 and 2024, and Australia's by 24%.

Twenty years in the top five

Australia has been one of the world's five biggest beef producers in almost every year since 2005. It briefly slipped to sixth place in 2021 and 2022, when farmers were holding on to cattle to rebuild their herds after the drought. Further down the list, Turkey and Pakistan have climbed into the top ten.

Where Australia's cattle live

Australia had about 27.6 million beef cattle in June 2025. Most of them are in the warm, grassy north-east: Queensland alone is home to about half of the national herd.

Boom and bust: the cattle cycle

Australia's herd is not steady. When rain falls, grass grows and farmers keep more cattle. When drought hits, there is not enough feed, so farmers sell cattle and the herd shrinks.

In 2014, about 80% of Queensland was officially in drought. A few years later, eastern Australia went through three dry years in a row, and 2019 was the driest year on record for the whole country. By June 2020 the herd had fallen to its lowest level in this 20-year record.

Beef cattle on farms in Australia at 30 June each year. Shaded areas mark major droughts. Sources: Australian Bureau of Statistics; Bureau of Meteorology; Queensland Government.

When the herd shrinks, more cattle go to market

Each square below is three months. The darker the square, the more cattle were slaughtered for meat. The drought years of 2014 and 2015 are dark, as farmers sold cattle they could not feed. In 2021 and 2022 the squares turn pale, because farmers were keeping cattle to rebuild their herds. By 2025, with herds rebuilt, Australia slaughtered 9.28 million cattle, the most since 1978.

The cattle cycle

This chart puts the two ideas together. Each dot is one year: further right means a bigger herd, higher up means more cattle sold. Follow the line from 2013 and it loops around: a big herd, then a drought sell-off, then a rebuild, then a big herd again. Farmers call this the cattle cycle.

Where the beef goes

Australia shipped about 1.55 million tonnes of beef overseas in 2025. Four countries took more than three-quarters of it: the United States, China, Japan and South Korea.

Malaysia received 10,876 tonnes, making it Australia's 13th-largest beef market. Thicker lines mean more beef. Hover over a line to see the details.

Beef and veal exports in 2025, in tonnes shipped weight. Shows the 16 countries that received at least 5,000 tonnes, plus Malaysia (together 97% of all exports). Lines end at each country's capital city. Source: Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry.

Ten years of change

Australia exported 20% more beef in 2025 than in 2015, but not every market grew. China's purchases almost doubled and Indonesia's rose by 70%. Japan, once the biggest buyer after the United States, now takes less. Malaysia also buys about 12% less Australian beef than it did ten years ago.

Beef and veal exports in 2015 and 2025 (k = thousand tonnes, shipped weight), for the five largest markets in 2025 plus Malaysia. Brown lines grew, grey lines fell, and Malaysia is shown in green. Hover over a line to highlight it. Source: Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry.

Why Malaysia buys beef from overseas

Some countries produce far more beef than they eat, and sell the rest abroad. Others eat more than they can produce, and must import the difference. In 2023, Malaysia produced about 39,000 tonnes of beef but used about 307,000 tonnes, so it made only about 13% of the beef it ate. Australia made about three times as much beef as it used.

Beef produced and beef used at home (production plus imports minus exports) in 2023, in thousand tonnes, for ten Asia-Pacific countries. China and the United States are left out because their much larger totals would squash the other countries. Includes buffalo meat. Source: FAOSTAT Food Balances.

A changing industry: more grain-fed beef

Most Australian cattle spend their lives grazing on open pasture. Beef from these animals is called grass-fed. Some cattle instead spend their final months in a feedlot, a fenced yard where they are fed a grain-based diet. Their beef is called grain-fed and is often more marbled (it has more fat running through the meat).

Grass-fed beef still makes up most of Australia's exports, but grain-fed beef is growing faster.
